About URL Encoding

URL encoding is just percent-encoding: any character that isn't safe in a URL becomes a "%" followed by two hex digits. A space turns into %20, "&" into %26, and so on. This tool wraps JavaScript's native encodeURIComponent() so you don't have to open a console to try it.

You need this any time a URL contains something you didn't type by hand — search terms, form values, redirect targets, anything with spaces or non-ASCII characters. Skip the encoding and the browser will mangle the URL, silently drop parameters, or both.

Since it all runs in your browser, there's nothing to upload — fine for URLs that embed tokens or private payloads.
